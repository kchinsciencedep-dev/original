Biology Mr Ruben

Biology is the study of living things and how they live, grow, reproduce and depend on their surroundings. Lessons mix theory with practical work such as using a microscope, testing foods and studying plants and animals.

What you study

  • Cells, tissues and how living things are classified
  • Nutrition, transport, gaseous exchange, respiration and excretion
  • Reproduction in plants and animals
  • Genetics and evolution
  • Ecology, the environment, health and disease

Key notes

  • The cell is the basic unit of life. Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole, which animal cells lack.
  • Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide and water, with light energy absorbed by chlorophyll, make glucose and oxygen.
  • Respiration releases energy from glucose. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and produces carbon dioxide and water.
  • Genes are sections of DNA that carry the instructions for inherited traits. A dominant allele shows even when only one copy is present.
  • In an ecosystem, energy flows from producers to consumers along food chains, and less energy is available at each step.

Practical skills

Using a light microscope, drawing and labelling specimens, testing foods for starch, sugars, proteins and fats, and recording observations accurately.

Chemistry Mr Murithi

Chemistry is the study of matter, what it is made of and how it changes. Lessons explain the reactions around us, from rusting and cooking to making soap, and include hands-on laboratory practicals.

What you study

  • Atoms, the periodic table and chemical bonding
  • The mole and chemical calculations
  • Acids, bases and salts
  • Rates of reaction and equilibrium
  • Oxidation, reduction and electrolysis
  • Carbon compounds, metals, non-metals and industrial processes

Key notes

  • Atoms have protons and neutrons in the nucleus and electrons in shells around it. The atomic number is the number of protons.
  • The pH scale runs from 0 to 14. Below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral and above 7 is alkaline.
  • One mole of any substance contains 6.02 × 1023 particles. Moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
  • Reactions go faster with a higher temperature, a higher concentration, a larger surface area or a catalyst.
  • Oxidation is loss of electrons and reduction is gain of electrons.

Practical skills

Titration, qualitative analysis (testing for ions and gases), handling apparatus safely, and recording burette readings and results in tables.

Physics Mr Lawi

Physics is the study of matter, energy, forces and how they interact. Lessons explain how everyday things work, from lights and machines to sound and heat, using measurements and experiments.

What you study

  • Measurement, motion and forces
  • Work, energy, power and machines
  • Heat and temperature
  • Light, waves and sound
  • Electricity and magnetism
  • Atomic and nuclear physics

Key notes

  • Speed = distance ÷ time. Acceleration is the change in velocity per unit of time.
  • Newton's laws: an object keeps its motion unless a force acts, force = mass × acceleration, and every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
  • Work done = force × distance moved. Power = work ÷ time, measured in watts.
  • Ohm's law: voltage = current × resistance (V = IR).
  • Light reflects at equal angles and bends (refracts) when it passes between materials such as air and glass.

Practical skills

Measuring with rulers, timers, ammeters and voltmeters, plotting graphs, and finding sources of error in an experiment.
